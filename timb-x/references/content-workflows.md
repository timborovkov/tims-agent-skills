# Content workflows

## Originals

Find the sentence that expresses the observation, result, or opinion. Lead with
that when it helps comprehension. Background earns space only if the reader needs
it. A useful structure might be an incident and its consequence, a failed attempt
and what changed, a supported disagreement, or an honest question with context.
These are options, not fill-in-the-blank hooks.

Use concrete proof when available: a real screenshot, measured result, example,
or reproducible detail. Don't replace a vague claim with an invented precise one.
Keep necessary source links where readers can use them; do not move every link
to a reply on the strength of an unsupported algorithm rule.

## Threads and long posts

Choose a thread when the material needs separate connected steps or the user requests
one. Choose a single long post only if the account supports it and that format
suits the request. Neither format requires a fixed segment count.

Make the opening state the actual point. Each segment should contribute a distinct
piece of evidence, explanation, or experience. Maintain a readable sequence and
avoid manufactured cliffhangers, repeated summaries, filler segments, and automatic
follow/bookmark/reply calls to action. End when the point is complete.

Verify the complete argument, not just individual sentences. A true statistic can
still fail to support the conclusion attached to it. Label forecasts as forecasts.

## Repurposing

Read the original material and identify the part relevant to the user's X audience.
Preserve the source's meaning and attribution. Do not convert someone else's
experience into the user's first-person story. Adapt length and framing without copying
another platform's sales pitch, hashtags, or formatting by default.

## Hook analysis

Read the actual reference post and, if available, its performance evidence. Explain
what draws interest: specificity, an unresolved question, recognizable experience,
or a clear disagreement. Separate the writing mechanism from the author's audience,
timing, subject, and existing reputation. Popularity alone does not establish why
it worked.

Transfer the useful principle to the user's own material, not the author's distinctive
phrasing or achievements. Reject a proposed hook if its payoff is absent. Treat
“make it viral” as a request to improve relevance and clarity, not a guarantee.

## Editing

Preserve the draft's intent and natural rhythm. Remove AI texture proportionally;
an already-good draft may need no changes. Do not replace ordinary language with
forced slang, deliberate typos, blanket lowercase, or mechanical short sentences.
Respect “grammar only”, “keep this wording”, and “post exactly this”.
