# Reach experiments

Use when Tim explicitly asks for more reach, growth, virality, or distribution
experiments. The main skill's voice and editorial rules remain authoritative.
Improve the chance of reaching relevant people without promising virality.

## Select a useful change

Infer the goal; clarify only when choosing between impressions, followers,
conversation, or customer interest materially changes the recommendation.
Identify the existing draft's audience and point before optimizing it.

For discovery and analytics, read [planning-and-learning.md](planning-and-learning.md).
It owns conversation selection, Tim's early results, and measurement standards.
For opening and format choices, use [content-workflows.md](content-workflows.md).
Do not load these references merely to make a small wording improvement.

Choose the most promising intervention rather than applying every tactic:

- **Opening or context:** Move the actual observation or supported result forward,
  or add the context an unfamiliar reader needs. The post must deliver its premise.
- **Format:** Consider whether a reply, quote, original, thread, or demonstration
  best carries the point to the intended audience. Explain a proposed format switch
  when it differs from the request.
- **Evidence:** Use an available screenshot, demonstration, or simple chart when
  it makes the point easier to understand or share, not as a presumed ranking bonus.
- **Timing:** Check whether the topic and conversation are current. Test posting
  times against account-specific evidence instead of prescribing universal hours.
- **Follow-through:** Answer substantive responses when there is something to add.
  Revisit older material when new evidence or a new angle earns another post.

Clearer or unchanged can be the best recommendation. Keep a natural brief reaction
brief. Check current primary sources before asserting ranking mechanics or platform
limits; otherwise describe the tactic as a hypothesis.

## Deliver the recommendation

Return the best draft plus one short sentence explaining the intended improvement.
Offer another version only for a meaningfully different approach or on request.
Do not attach invented virality scores or projected impression counts.

For reply discovery, provide worthwhile targets with URLs, context, and the
contribution Tim could make. Apply the main skill's quality-over-quantity rule.

## Design an experiment

1. Separate the observed result from the proposed explanation, using the evidence
   standards in planning-and-learning.md.
2. Change one meaningful variable where practical: opening, format, topic
   selection, evidence, or timing. Keep the claim and voice intact.
3. Choose a goal-relevant metric and comparable observation window before reviewing
   the outcome. Use the planning reference's comparison and missing-data rules.
4. Recommend continuing, revising, or dropping the tactic based on the results.
   Do not call unrelated posts a controlled A/B test or treat a breakout as proof.

An experiment is a proposal, not permission to publish or start an automation.
Follow the main skill's execution boundary for external actions.
